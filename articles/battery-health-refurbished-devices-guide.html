Battery Health and Refurbished Devices: What Every Buyer and Seller Should Know

Standard Mobile Company October 2026 6 min read

Ask refurbished-device buyers what they worry about most and battery health now tops the list — often ahead of cosmetics. It makes sense: a scratch is visible and forgivable, but a battery that dies by lunchtime feels like being sold a lemon. For refurbishers and resellers, battery policy has quietly become one of the biggest drivers of returns, reviews, and repeat business.

What “battery health” actually measures

Battery health is a measure of a battery’s current maximum capacity relative to when it was new, expressed as a percentage. A phone reporting 100% holds a full original charge; one at 80% holds roughly four-fifths of it. Health declines with charge cycles and age, and the rate varies by device, usage, and heat exposure.

It’s important to separate capacity (how long it lasts per charge) from condition (whether the battery is safe). A swollen or overheating battery is a safety issue regardless of its reported percentage, and should always be replaced.

The thresholds that matter

  • 85–100% — generally sells as-is with confidence; disclose the figure.
  • 80–85% — acceptable for many channels, but buyers increasingly expect disclosure or replacement.
  • Below 80% — most marketplaces and discerning buyers expect a replacement; many platforms set 80% as a floor for “refurbished” grades.
  • Any swelling or fault — replace, no exceptions.
A battery replacement is cheap compared to a return, a one-star review, and a lost repeat customer. Treat battery policy as a sales decision, not just a cost.

Replace or disclose?

There are two defensible strategies, and the right one depends on your channel:

  1. Replace to a standard — set a minimum (commonly 85% or 90%) and replace anything below it. This produces consistent, premium inventory and minimizes returns, at a higher per-unit cost.
  2. Test and disclose — accurately measure and publish the health figure for each unit, letting price reflect it. This works well for value-tier buyers who prize transparency and a lower price.

What you should never do is stay silent. Undisclosed weak batteries are the fastest route to returns and reputation damage.

Why documentation wins

Whether you replace or disclose, the batteries you can document are the ones that protect you. Per-unit battery health readings, captured during functional testing, let you set accurate listings, defend against disputes, and build the kind of consistency buyers come back for. In a market where trust is the scarcest commodity, a documented battery number is a small thing that closes a lot of sales.
