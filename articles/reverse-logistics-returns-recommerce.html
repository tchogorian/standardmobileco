Reverse Logistics: How to Manage Returns in Recommerce Without Losing Your Margin

Standard Mobile Company October 2026 6 min read

In recommerce, returns are where margin quietly disappears. A returned device isn’t just a refund — it’s inbound freight, inspection labor, re-grading, repackaging, and often a markdown. Handle returns reactively and they erode profitability; handle them with a real reverse-logistics process and they become a recovery opportunity.

Why returns are so costly

A single return can touch a dozen steps: authorization, inbound shipping, receiving, inspection, data wiping, functional testing, cosmetic re-grading, repackaging, and relisting — each with a cost. Many returned devices have nothing wrong with them (“changed my mind,” “wrong model”), yet they can’t be resold as new. Without a disciplined path, they pile up, age, and lose value by the week.

The foundation: fast, accurate triage

The first thing a returned unit needs is a decision. Effective triage sorts returns quickly into clear buckets:

  • Resell as-is — functionally and cosmetically fine; relist, often as open-box.
  • Light refurbishment — needs cleaning, a battery, a minor part, or re-grading.
  • Full repair — a genuine fault worth fixing given resale value.
  • Harvest or recycle — beyond economical repair; recover parts or responsibly recycle.

The faster and more accurately you triage, the less value decays in limbo.

Most returned devices aren’t broken. The money is lost not in the fault, but in the time and friction between return and relist.

Disposition is a pricing decision

Each bucket has an optimal channel. Open-box units may return to the same marketplace at a modest discount. Lightly refurbished devices move to value tiers. Repaired units re-enter mainstream refurbished channels. Matching disposition to channel — rather than dumping everything into one liquidation pile — is where recovery rates are won or lost.

Data and documentation still apply

A returned device is a data-bearing device. Every return needs the same sanitization and chain-of-custody discipline as any other unit before it’s resold. Building data handling into the returns flow — not bolting it on later — keeps you compliant and protects the buyer.

When to outsource the reverse flow

Returns are spiky and labor-intensive, which makes them a natural candidate for a processing partner. If returns are overwhelming your bench, aging in a corner, or getting liquidated wholesale, a partner who can triage, refurbish, and re-grade at volume often recovers far more than the processing costs — turning your returns pile from a drag on margin back into inventory.
