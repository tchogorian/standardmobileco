How to Choose a Refurbishing Partner: 7 Questions to Ask Before You Ship a Single Unit

Standard Mobile Company October 21, 2026 7 min read

Handing your inventory to a refurbishing partner is an act of trust. You’re shipping devices that still hold recoverable value, often along with data-bearing assets, to a facility you may never physically visit. Get the partner right and you unlock margin you were leaving on the table. Get it wrong and you lose units, time, and sometimes your reputation with your own customers.

Most bad partnerships could have been avoided with better questions up front. Below are the seven that matter most — the ones that separate a partner you can build volume with from one that becomes an expensive lesson.

1. “What happens to my data-bearing devices?”

This is the first question for a reason. Any phone, tablet, or laptop that passed through an end user is a potential data-liability. Ask exactly how the partner sanitizes storage, which standard they follow (NIST 800-88 is the baseline you want to hear), and whether you receive per-device documentation. If the answer is vague, or if certified data destruction is treated as an upsell rather than a default, walk away. A serious partner treats data handling as non-negotiable.

2. “Can you handle my mix — not just your favorite SKUs?”

Plenty of refurbishers will happily take clean, high-demand, single-SKU lots. The real test is what happens with mixed ASINs, odd models, and hard-to-repair units. Those are exactly the devices that destroy your margin when they get kicked back or routed to liquidation. A capable partner should be comfortable quoting the whole lot — the easy and the ugly — and be transparent about expected repair yield on the tough stuff.

The partner who only wants your clean inventory isn’t a partner. They’re a cherry-picker, and you keep the losses.

3. “How do you grade, and can I see the criteria in writing?”

“Grade B” means nothing until you see the definition behind it. Ask for the written grading rubric: cosmetic thresholds, functional test coverage, battery health minimums, and how borderline units are classified. You want grading criteria that match the marketplaces you sell into — Amazon Renewed, eBay Refurbished, carrier, or retail. Consistent, documented grading is what lets you forecast recovery before a single unit ships.

4. “What is your repair yield, and who eats the cost of a failed repair?”

Repair yield — the percentage of units that come out the other side sellable — is the number that actually drives your economics. Ask for realistic yield ranges by category and condition, and get clarity on the commercial model: do you pay per unit attempted, or per unit successfully refurbished? Who absorbs parts and labor on a device that can’t be saved? Ambiguity here is where surprise invoices come from.

5. “Can I see each unit’s status while it’s in your facility?”

Visibility is the difference between a vendor and a black box. You should be able to track your inventory at the unit level — received, in repair, graded, shipped — along with the costs attached to each stage. If a partner can’t tell you where your devices are or what’s happening to them, you’re flying blind, and reconciling a shipment of thousands of units after the fact becomes a nightmare.

6. “What compliance and certifications stand behind your process?”

Compliance is both risk management and a sales asset. Ask about R2 processes, environmental handling, and whether the partner can produce documentation your enterprise and government clients will demand. Be precise with language: “R2 compliant” (operating to the standard’s practices) is different from “R2 certified” (holding the audited certificate). A trustworthy partner will state exactly which applies to them rather than blur the two.

7. “How fast can you quote, and how do I get a straight answer?”

Speed and clarity in the quoting process tell you a lot about how the whole relationship will run. A partner who takes weeks to price a lot, or who hides behind vague “it depends” answers, will be just as slow and vague when something goes wrong mid-project. You want a clear turnaround commitment and a single point of contact who can give you real numbers.

Putting it together

Run these seven questions past any refurbisher before you commit volume. The good ones answer directly, in writing, and without flinching at your toughest inventory. The ones who dodge, upsell the basics, or only want your easy SKUs are telling you exactly how the relationship will go.

Choosing a partner is ultimately about reducing surprises: fewer surprise costs, fewer surprise rejections, fewer surprise data risks. Ask hard questions now so your inventory — and your margin — are in steady hands later.
