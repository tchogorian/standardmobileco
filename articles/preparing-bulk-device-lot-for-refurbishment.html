How to Prepare a Bulk Device Lot for Refurbishment (and Get a Faster, Better Quote)

Standard Mobile Company October 2026 6 min read

The quality of the quote you get back on a bulk device lot is largely determined before you ever ship it. A clean, well-documented lot gets priced quickly and accurately; a mystery pallet gets priced conservatively, because the refurbisher has to price in uncertainty. Here is how to prepare a lot so the numbers come back fast and in your favor.

1. Build a real manifest

The single highest-leverage thing you can do is provide an accurate manifest. At minimum, capture:

  • Make and model — be specific (e.g. “iPhone 13 128GB,” not “iPhone”).
  • Quantity per model.
  • Known condition / fault — working, cracked screen, won’t power on, liquid damage, unknown.
  • Lock status if known — carrier lock, activation lock, blacklist.

A spreadsheet (CSV or XLSX) is ideal because it can be priced line by line. Even a rough manifest beats none — it lets a partner quote the lot instead of guessing.

2. Be honest about condition

It’s tempting to describe a lot optimistically, but it backfires. Refurbishers verify on intake, and a lot that arrives worse than described triggers requotes, delays, and eroded trust. Accurate condition reporting — including the ugly units — produces a quote you can actually rely on and a relationship that scales.

A conservative but honest manifest earns a better quote than an optimistic one that falls apart on intake.

3. Separate the obvious categories

If feasible, physically or logically separate major categories: phones from tablets from laptops, working from non-working, locked from clean. You don’t need lab-grade sorting — even rough segregation speeds intake and improves pricing accuracy.

4. Package to survive the trip

Damage in transit is lost value that nobody recovers. Protect screens, use adequate cushioning, palletize and shrink-wrap for freight, and avoid loose devices rattling in oversized boxes. For data-bearing devices, keep chain-of-custody intact from your dock onward.

5. Flag data and compliance needs up front

Tell your partner what you need on data destruction (NIST 800-88 level), what documentation your end client requires, and any export or compliance constraints. Surfacing this at quote time avoids surprises and rework later.

6. Send it all at once

Bundle the manifest, condition notes, photos of representative units, and your requirements into a single submission. The more complete the package, the faster and tighter the quote. A good partner should be able to turn a well-prepared lot into a full project quote — logistics, parts, repair, and projected yield — within a few days.

The payoff

Ten minutes of preparation on your end can shave days off the quote cycle and meaningfully improve the numbers. Treat the manifest as the foundation of the deal, because that’s exactly what it is.
